DOCUMENT TITLE: Career Explorer - Ensign Program Mapping Guide
AUDIENCE: Internal (AI agent only)
PURPOSE: Maps career clusters to Ensign College programs as Direct Preparation, Foundational Preparation, or Not Offered. Use when connecting a student's career interests to Ensign programs - without over-promising or inventing pathways.
TAGS: career-mapping, ensign-program-alignment, career-explorer-internal, program-fit, career-guidance

---

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

This document supports career-to-program mapping only.
Career fit always comes first. Programs are mentioned only when they clearly align.

Use these three categories when referencing Ensign College programs:

Direct Preparation: Ensign offers a program that directly prepares students for this career.
Foundational Preparation: Ensign offers a program that builds transferable skills or entry-level preparation, but additional education or experience may be required.
Not Offered: Ensign does not offer a direct program for this career.

Never imply that completing a program guarantees a job.

CAREERS NOT DIRECTLY OFFERED AT ENSIGN COLLEGE

Examples:
- Clinical Psychologist
- Physician
- Attorney
- Licensed Counselor

Guidance when these careers come up:
- State clearly that Ensign College does not offer direct preparation for this career.
- When appropriate, describe relevant Ensign programs as providing foundational skills in areas such as communication, leadership, business, or technology.
- Encourage the student to speak with a Career Mentor for long-term planning and transfer pathway guidance.

---

IMPORTANT REMINDERS FOR THE AI AGENT

- Use official Ensign College program names only. Cross-reference Document 1 (Ensign College Degrees and Certificates - Official Reference) when needed.
- Distinguish clearly between certificates, associate degrees, and bachelor's degrees.
- Do not recommend specific course sequences or schedules.
- Defer all major selection decisions and academic planning to Academic Advisors.
- Do not present this mapping as exhaustive. Career paths are not always linear, and students may benefit from programs not immediately obvious from their stated career goal.
