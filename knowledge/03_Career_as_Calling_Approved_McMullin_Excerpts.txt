APPROVED CORE IDEA: A CAREER IS MORE THAN A JOB

Direct Quote (approved for use):
"A career is more than a job... A career sets forth a person's progress or general course of action through life. It is what you become as a result of what you do."

Context: McMullin draws on Webster's dictionary to reframe "career" as something closer to a calling - a life's direction, not a job title.

Safe Agent Paraphrase:
"As one Ensign College speaker put it, a career is not just what you do - it's what you become. It sets the course of your life, which makes the choices you make now more significant than they might seem."

---

APPROVED CORE IDEA: CAREER AS LIFE'S MISSION

Direct Quote (approved for use):
"You are here at the outset of that mission in a very real way because you are pursuing an education that is to help you in the progress of your life."

Context: McMullin frames enrollment at Ensign College as the beginning of a life's mission, not merely job preparation.

Safe Agent Paraphrase:
"Your time at Ensign College is not just about earning a credential. It is the beginning of something larger - a life's mission that your career will help shape."

---

APPROVED CORE IDEA: CAREER AS CALLING - FAITH AND PREPARATION

Direct Quote (approved for use):
"Decide that your career is your mission, not your job. Your mission comes first. Your job comes along and everything works out all right."

Context: McMullin responds to a student question about balancing professional responsibilities with spiritual ones. He argues that mission-first thinking protects against the pressures of employer demands.

Safe Agent Paraphrase:
"One way to think about this: your career is your calling, not just your job. When your sense of purpose leads, the work tends to follow in a healthier and more sustainable way."

---

APPROVED CORE IDEA: DAILY HABITS SHAPE CAREER DIRECTION

Direct Quote (approved for use):
"I have found in my own particular instance that daily routines influence your career... those influences have shaped the way I view myself in relationship to others around me as well as to my responsibilities in the outside world."

Context: McMullin describes three personal habits - spiritual preparation, personal discipline (making his bed), and choosing integrity over convenience - as the foundation of his career.

Safe Agent Paraphrase:
"Career direction is often shaped less by a single big decision and more by the small, consistent choices you make daily - how you prepare, how you show up, and what you are willing to do when no one is watching."

---

APPROVED CORE IDEA: TAKE THE ROAD LESS TRAVELED

Reference (Robert Frost, cited by McMullin - do not quote as McMullin's own words):
McMullin referenced Robert Frost's poem "The Road Not Taken" and applied it to career choices: choosing integrity, truth, and faith over the path of least resistance or worldly expectation.

McMullin Direct Quote (approved for use):
"Take the road less traveled by the world, and it makes all the difference in your career. Decide the Lord's way instead of the worldly way."

Safe Agent Paraphrase (for spiritually receptive students only):
"Career choices often involve deciding between what is easy or expected and what truly aligns with your values. Choosing with integrity, even when it is harder, tends to shape careers that feel meaningful over time."

USAGE GUIDELINES FOR THE AI AGENT

Use these excerpts only when:
1. The student has indicated openness to faith-based or values-based framing.
2. The conversation has moved into the Faith Integration section of the workflow.
3. The student is exploring questions of purpose, meaning, or career direction - not just program selection.

Do not use these excerpts when:
- The student is asking a factual question about programs, schedules, or assessments.
- The student has not indicated a faith context or has expressed discomfort with religious framing.
- The conversation is in an early, information-gathering phase.

Always present faith-related language as one lens among several - not as a requirement.

ATTRIBUTION GUIDANCE

When quoting McMullin directly, use: "As Ensign College speaker Keith McMullin put it..."
When paraphrasing, no attribution is required.
Do not introduce McMullin by title, position, or church affiliation unless the student asks.
