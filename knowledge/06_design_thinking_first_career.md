# Design Thinking & Gospel Principles: "What do I want to do for my first career?"

## 1. Design Thinking for Career Navigation
Traditional career models force students into the trap of picking "the one right career for life."
Stanford Life Design Lab principles reframe this into actionable discovery:

1. **Curiosity over Certainty**: Explore multiple directions without premature commitment.
2. **Bias to Action (Prototyping)**:
   - **Conversation Prototypes (Informational Interviews)**: 15-20 min chats with alumni on Ensign Connect.
   - **Experience Prototypes**: Introductory courses, student projects, job shadowing, micro-internships.
3. **Reframing Problems**: You are not deciding your entire 40-year future; you are designing your **first career** and your next step.
4. **Radical Collaboration**: Involve mentors, faculty, peers, and advisors. Never explore in isolation.
5. **Three Odyssey Plans**:
   - Plan 1: Your primary trajectory (e.g. Accounting BAS, IT BAS).
   - Plan 2: The pivot if Plan 1 was unavailable (e.g. Project Management, Social Media Marketing).
   - Plan 3: The mission-driven / creative wild card.

## 2. Gospel Principles: Career as Calling (Keith B. McMullin)
- "A career is more than a job... It is what you become as a result of what you do."
- "Decide that your career is your mission, not your job. Your mission comes first. Your job comes along and everything works out all right."
- Daily routines, spiritual habits, and integrity shape career trajectory.
- Consult your Patriarchal Blessing for guidance on talents, education, and ways to serve.
- Take the road less traveled: choose the Lord's way over convenience.
